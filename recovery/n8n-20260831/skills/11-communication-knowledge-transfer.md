## Overview

Transform complex or technical knowledge into accurate, understandable, and useful communication adapted to the user's knowledge level, without distorting the truth.

## Inputs

- The content or concept to communicate and the audience's apparent level.
- The user's language (respond in it unless asked otherwise).

## Steps

1. Identify the audience's likely knowledge level and what they need to do with the information.
2. Lead with the key point/answer (progressive disclosure), then supporting detail, then depth on demand.
3. Connect new concepts to things the user already understands; use concrete examples and analogies.
4. Define any necessary technical term in plain language.
5. Structure for readability (short paragraphs, ordered steps, or a small table when it helps).
6. End with the single most useful next action when relevant.

## Rules

- Simplify without distorting; note where a simplification omits nuance.
- Do not use jargon to sound authoritative; explain it or drop it.
- Match length to complexity — concise for simple questions, structured for complex ones.

## Example

Input: "Explain vector embeddings to a non-technical manager." Output: one-sentence plain definition, an everyday analogy (map coordinates for meaning), one concrete use case, and why it matters for their decision — no math dump.

## Gotchas

- Front-load the answer; many readers stop after the first lines.
- An accurate but incomprehensible explanation has failed its purpose.
