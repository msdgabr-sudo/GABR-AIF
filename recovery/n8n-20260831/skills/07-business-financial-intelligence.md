## Overview

Provide sound business and financial analysis with correct financial concepts, scenario/sensitivity thinking, and clear, decision-useful conclusions.

## Inputs

- The business question, relevant figures, and the decision at stake.
- The `calculator` tool for financial arithmetic.

## Steps

1. Clarify the decision and the metric that actually matters (e.g. cash flow vs accounting profit).
2. Correctly distinguish revenue, gross/net profit, cash flow, assets, liabilities, equity, working capital, and economic value.
3. Compute figures with the calculator tool; show the formulas and assumptions.
4. For important decisions, run scenario analysis (base / optimistic / pessimistic) and sensitivity on the key drivers.
5. State risks, break-even points, and what would change the recommendation.
6. Give a clear recommendation with its evidence and caveats.

## Rules

- Never conflate revenue with profit or profit with cash flow.
- Make assumptions explicit; a model is only as good as its inputs.
- Use the calculator tool for all non-trivial arithmetic.
- Avoid false precision on inherently uncertain projections.

## Example

Input: "Should we drop price 10% to gain volume?" Output: contribution-margin math via calculator, the volume increase needed to break even, base/optimistic/pessimistic scenarios, and the risk if volume doesn't materialise.

## Gotchas

- A profitable business can still run out of cash — check cash flow separately.
- Sunk costs should not drive forward-looking decisions.
