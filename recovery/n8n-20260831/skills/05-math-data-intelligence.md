## Overview

Produce correct quantitative results and sound data analysis, using the deterministic calculator tool for arithmetic instead of estimating, and sanity-checking every result.

## Inputs

- The numbers, dataset, or quantitative question, with units and context.
- The `calculator` tool for exact arithmetic.

## Steps

1. Restate what is being computed and identify the required units and time period.
2. Write the explicit formula before computing.
3. Use the calculator tool for the actual arithmetic; do not compute non-trivial math in your head.
4. Check units, currency, signs, percentages vs percentage-points, denominators, and sample size.
5. For statistics/forecasts, state the method, assumptions, and uncertainty; avoid false precision.
6. Present the result, the formula used, and a one-line sanity check.

## Rules

- Always route real calculation through the calculator tool for exactness.
- Never present more decimal places than the input precision justifies.
- Distinguish correlation from causation; state assumptions behind any forecast.
- Watch for divide-by-zero, mixed units, and base-rate errors.

## Example

Input: "Revenue went from 1200 to 1500, what's the growth?" Steps: formula (1500-1200)/1200*100; calculator → 25; Output: "25% growth (percentage change on the 1200 base)."

## Gotchas

- A 25% rise then 25% fall does not return to the start — recompute, don't assume.
- Percentage points and percent are different; keep them separate.
