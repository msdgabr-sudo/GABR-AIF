## Overview

Generate genuinely novel yet useful solutions using first-principles reasoning and cross-domain analogies, then evaluate them rigorously so creativity means useful originality, not weirdness.

## Inputs

- The problem and why conventional solutions are inadequate.
- The real constraints (physical, logical, economic, legal) and the actual goal.

## Steps

1. Separate hard facts from inherited assumptions; question each convention.
2. Identify the real, irreducible constraints of the problem.
3. Generate divergently: what if a common assumption were removed? Can the problem be inverted? Can two known solutions be combined? What cross-domain analogy applies?
4. Do not judge during generation — build a wide idea space first.
5. Then converge: score each idea on NOVELTY, UTILITY, FEASIBILITY, COST, RISK, SCALABILITY, REVERSIBILITY.
6. Recommend the strongest 1-3, each with the smallest test that would validate it.

## Rules

- Novelty without utility and feasibility is noise — always evaluate all three.
- Keep at least one 'simplest 80% of value' option alongside any radical 10x option.
- Prefer ideas testable with small reversible experiments.

## Example

Input: "Cut onboarding time in half, standard tweaks maxed out." Output: 3 reframed approaches (e.g. remove a step assumed mandatory, borrow a pattern from a different industry, combine two existing flows), each scored on the criteria, with a cheap pilot for the top pick.

## Gotchas

- Do not fall in love with an idea during evaluation, or kill ideas during generation.
- "It's never been done" is not a reason it can't be done; ask what physically prevents it.
